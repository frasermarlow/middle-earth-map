The Silmarillion on the map of Middle-earth

The widest spread of any book here — from the far eastern shore where the Elves first woke, to an island in the western sea that by the Third Age no longer existed.

These are the places from The Silmarillion and the elder days that this map can show. You can open the map with only these places marked, or find them among the rest in the full gazetteer.

One thing is worth saying plainly, because it explains why the list is short. This is a Third Age map. Beleriand — the stage for almost all of The Silmarillion, with Doriath, Gondolin, Nargothrond and the rest — lay west of the Blue Mountains, and was broken and drowned in the War of Wrath at the First Age’s end. It is not off to one side of this map; it is under the sea beyond its western edge. What survived are peaks: the map carries Himling, once Himring where Maedhros held his fortress, and Tol Fuin, all that is left above water of Dorthonion. Both are filed under Appendices rather than here.

What does fall inside the map spans the whole legendarium, edge to edge. Cuiviénen, where the Elves awoke, sits at x 7091, at the far eastern limit of the parchment; the Meneltarma of Númenor sits at x 237, out in the western sea. Between them: Durin waking at Khazad-dûm, the Dwarven cities of Ered Luin, the founding of Lindon and the Grey Havens after Beleriand’s ruin, the forging of the Rings in Eregion and the One in Mount Doom, the raising of Barad-dûr, the Corsair haven at Umbar, the Last Alliance on Dagorlad, and the loss of the Ring at the Gladden Fields.

It is the only book on this map whose events run through all three Ages — which is the point of it. For the Third Age story that grows out of these events, start with The Hobbit or open the full map.
